# Product

## Register

brand

> Primary register is **brand** — the public marketing / SEO layer (homepage, guides, pricing) is where design *is* the product and is impeccable's written default. The authenticated **dashboard app** (feeds, editors, onboarding) is the second register: design *serves* the task there. Both surfaces share one design system; override to `product` per task when working inside the app.

## Users

Primarily parents, adult children, and families with young kids — the generation that holds the family's living memory and feels the quiet pressure to keep it before it's lost. Grandparents can be invited in, but the product is not built older-users-first.

They arrive with a hundred stories and nowhere to put them: the recipe no one wrote down, the voice on an old voicemail, the reason behind a photo. Their context is domestic and unhurried — an evening, a quiet moment, a prompt to "ask Mum a question." The job to be done: **capture the stories, voices, recipes, and small everyday moments a family carries but never records, so they survive the handoff from one generation to the next** — and know, with confidence, that they're safe.

## Product Purpose

Ancestorii is a private family memory platform. It exists to preserve the story *behind* the photo and the voice *telling* it — kept safe for children and grandchildren to return to in ten, twenty, fifty years — and to turn those memories into physical heirlooms.

What it is **not**, deliberately:
- Not a public social network.
- Not a genealogy / family-tree records tool.
- Not a photo dump.

It's for the family that has a hundred stories but nowhere to put them. Success looks like a family that keeps coming back to add one more memory, trusts the platform with what's irreplaceable, and one day hands the result down.

The surface splits in two, sharing one system:
- **Public marketing layer** — earns the trust and explains the promise before anyone signs up. Design carries the persuasion.
- **Authenticated app** — the private space (My Family feed, timelines, albums, capsules, editors) where the capturing actually happens, plus the print pipeline that produces the heirlooms.

## Brand Personality

**Warm, trustworthy, enduring — expressed through clean modern craft, not sentimental decoration.**

The emotional target on every screen is **calm and trust**: safe, private, unhurried; the user should feel their family's memories are protected and taken seriously. Crucially, that feeling is delivered through a clean, confident, modern interface — *not* through soft or sentimental visual texture. Warmth lives in the copy and in real family photography; it is never applied as a muddy palette or decorative flourish.

Voice: warm and human, action-led ("add a memory", "ask Mum a question"), British English throughout. Confident, never precious. It reassures by being clear, not by being soft.

## Anti-references

What Ancestorii must **not** look or feel like:

1. **Literal "heirloom" styling — the #1 thing to avoid.** No cream / parchment backgrounds, muddy or washed-out gold, ornate filigree, low-contrast sepia tones. The heirloom is the *outcome*, not the interface.
2. **Dated genealogy tools** (Ancestry, MyHeritage) — clinical charts, records-database coldness, grey administrative UI.
3. **Morbid memorial / funeral-home heaviness** — sombre, grief-forward. This is about life carried forward, not mourning.
4. **Craft-store scrapbook kitsch** — stickers, washi tape, novelty fonts, twee decoration that undercuts the sense of something worth keeping.
5. **Dark / "Jarvis" / sci-fi tech aesthetic** — no dark mode as the default, no neon, no black-glass dashboards.

**Positive direction:** clean and modern, like a well-designed SaaS product — pure white, strong confident colour, high contrast, generous space, crisp type. Warmth comes from words and real family photography, never from a muted palette.

## Design Principles

1. **Warmth in the words, not the walls.** Emotional warmth is carried by copy and real family photography. The interface itself stays clean, high-contrast, and confident — never softened with sentimental texture, muddy tones, or decoration.
2. **Confidence signals safety.** For a product holding what's irreplaceable, a crisp, modern, well-crafted surface *is* the trust signal. Calm is earned through clarity and craft, not through hush.
3. **The story is the subject.** The product exists for the story behind the photo and the voice telling it. Design recedes so family content leads; chrome never competes with what the family put in.
4. **Built to outlive the trend.** Enduring over fashionable. Avoid both dated genealogy/memorial tropes and fleeting tech-aesthetic fads; choose choices that will still read as considered in a decade.
5. **One system, two registers.** Marketing (brand) and app (product) share tokens, type, and colour discipline. The seam between "learn about it" and "use it" should feel continuous, not like two different products.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.1 AA, met cleanly** across both surfaces. The clean, high-contrast, generously-spaced direction already delivers legible type and strong contrast, so AA should fall out of the design rather than being retrofitted.

No older-user-first optimisation is required — the primary audience is parents and adult children — but nothing should exclude an invited grandparent: honour `prefers-reduced-motion` on the motion-heavy marketing layer, keep tap targets generous, and never rely on colour alone to convey meaning.
